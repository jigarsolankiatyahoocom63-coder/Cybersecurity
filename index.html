Why this matters

Most risks begin with small, everyday habits

Weak passwords

Simple passwords are still the easiest route for attackers to reuse across accounts.

Phishing traps

Urgent messages and fake links often look legitimate, but they pressure you into sharing information.

Oversharing

Public profiles reveal personal details that can be used for scams, impersonation, and account recovery attacks.

Unsafe financial habits

Unverified payment requests, suspicious downloads, and fake shopping pages can lead to fraud.

Quick actions

Test your habits today

Quick challenge 1: Password strength

Passwords that are short, predictable, or reused create weak points across all your accounts. Changing one weak password is not enough; the safer habit is to create unique, strong passwords and store them in a password manager.

Open the password checker

Quick challenge 2: Personal data protection

Your information becomes more valuable when shared publicly. Reduce risk by limiting personal details and checking permissions on apps and social accounts.

Use the checklist

Quick challenge 3: Spot the threat

Before you click, pause. Ask whether the message is expected, whether the sender is trustworthy, and whether the link uses a legitimate domain. Real organisations do not pressure you to act without verifying.
